About Letonism

Letonism is a monotheistic religious tradition devoted to the worship of the One God, Elvé.

Letonists seek to live according to the teachings of the sacred scriptures, maintain prayer and religious practice, observe the commandments, and cultivate a life of gratitude, responsibility, and righteousness.

The Letonism Organization exists to support Letonists in their religious lives and to preserve and communicate the teachings and traditions of Letonism.

The Life of a Letonist

Faith

Faith in the One God Elvé forms the foundation of Letonist religious life.

Scripture

Letonists study the sacred scriptures and seek to understand their teachings.

Prayer

Prayer is practiced as an expression of gratitude, devotion, reflection, and remembrance of God.

Commandments

Religious commandments provide guidance for daily conduct and ethical living.

The Revelation Pentateuch

The Revelation Pentateuch is the collective name for the five sacred scriptures of Letonism.

These scriptures contain teachings concerning faith, worship, morality, commandments, religious practice, and the proper way of life for Letonists.

The Five Sacred Scriptures

The five books form the central scriptural corpus of Letonism and are studied individually as well as as a unified tradition.

The scriptures are regarded as sacred writings that guide Letonists in understanding God and living according to the religious tradition.

The Letonic Language

Letonic is the historical language of the Leton people and is also used in religious and cultural contexts.

The language is written primarily using the Latin alphabet, with additional letters including ŋ and ʔ.

Letonic has an agglutinative grammatical structure and uses case marking on nouns. Its basic word order is Subject–Object–Verb (SOV).

The study of Letonic provides an additional way to explore Leton culture, religious terminology, scripture, and historical tradition.
